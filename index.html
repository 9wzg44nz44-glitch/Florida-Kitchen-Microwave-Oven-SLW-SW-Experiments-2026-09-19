1 · Hypothesis we are testing

A consumer microwave oven is already a strong Faraday cage at 2.45 GHz (TEM leakage is tightly regulated). Extended Electrodynamics / Hively-style bookkeeping asks whether any non-TEM component (Scalar Longitudinal Wave SLW or Scalar Wave SW) could still be detectable outside or through soft Faraday cloth when the oven is on — or whether what we see is ordinary TEM leakage, multipath, or instrument artifact.

Today’s kitchen work is an effective mockup / first listen: TinySA Ultras with two antenna types, soft Faraday-cloth bag sealed by multi-wrap + kitchen clips, oven on/off baselines. It maps to the longer lab proposal on the Hively → microwave-oven Faraday-cage methods page, and also informs Experiment A packing (sphere / mesh enclosure discipline).

Warm skepticism. A peak on a TinySA is not “proven SLW.” Prefer: sealed cloth, source-off baselines, antenna type A/B comparison, and a magnetic (H) probe B-null before any SLW/SW-vs-TEM language.

Bifilar hybrid coil (TinySA #2)

Two opposing-wind single-wire pancake coils, electrically joined at the center (coil end of the wire), laid on top of each other so the currents are opposing. Intent: the magnetic fields largely cancel (TEM / loop-like B response suppressed), leaving a detector more sensitive to hypothesized longitudinal / scalar-ish coupling than a plain monopole — still HYP, not a proof.

Cloth seal (important)

Edges are not left open for the real runs: three wraps of fabric-on-fabric, held shut with kitchen thumb/finger clips (same family as cereal-bag clips). Seal first; grounding is optional.

4 · Next · H10 magnetic probe (B-null)

Strong next step: place Tekbox H10 (or H20) inside the Faraday cloth beside the RX antennas with the microwave on, and ask whether a clear B-field accompanies any SA peak.

  • SA peak + clear H-probe line → treat as TEM / mixed — do not claim pure SLW/SW.
  • SA peak + H at noise → consistent with weak-B hypothesis; still not a proof (log orientations, LNA, seal).
